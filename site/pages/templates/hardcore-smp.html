The test server's server.properties read hardcore=true, difficulty=hard and pvp=false.

{{template "h2n" (dict "ID" "death" "N" 2 "Text" "What hardcore means on a server" "Toc" "What death means")}}
  • One life. A player who dies turns into a spectator: they can fly around and watch, but not play.
  • The world goes on. Unlike singleplayer hardcore, nothing is deleted, and everyone still alive keeps playing.
  • Hard, always. Mobs hit harder and hunger can kill, and the difficulty can't be turned down while hardcore is on.
{{template "h2n" (dict "ID" "revive" "N" 3 "Text" "Bringing someone back" "Toc" "Bring someone back")}}

Some groups allow it once. In the Console, move them somewhere safe first, since a spectator can be inside a wall, then give them survival back:

tp Steve Alex
gamemode survival Steve

Or restore the backup from before the fatal fight. It brings the world and every player back as they were then; Playkeeper's Back up now takes one without stopping the server.

{{template "h2n" (dict "ID" "prepare" "N" 4 "Text" "Before the first night" "Toc" "Before the first night")}}
  • Pre-generate the world on the World tab, which runs Chunky: lag on hard difficulty gets people killed.
  • Talk. Simple Voice Chat uses UDP 24454, which Playkeeper opens on the machine; open it in your provider's firewall too, and friends add the mod.
  • Back up before big fights. The Nether, the End or a raid are where most runs end.
